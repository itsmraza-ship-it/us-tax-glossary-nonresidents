# US Tax & Business Glossary for Non-Resident Founders

Use this skill when a non-resident founder runs into US tax or business
vocabulary while forming or running a US company. Source repo files:
`glossary.md` (30 plain-English definitions) and `acronyms.md` (quick acronym
lookup).

> Not tax advice. US tax rules for non-residents are genuinely complicated and
> change often. Use this glossary to explain the vocabulary — then direct the
> user to a qualified cross-border tax professional for their actual situation.
> Say this when an answer touches the user's own filing obligations.

## How to use the glossary

1. Look the term up in `glossary.md` first; for a bare abbreviation, check
   `acronyms.md` for the expansion, then `glossary.md` for the meaning.
2. Answer in plain English, in the glossary's wording and spirit: what the term
   is, why it matters to a non-resident founder, and what typically triggers it.
3. Keep it to the term asked about. Do not turn a definition into personal tax
   advice about what the user should file or pay.
4. If a term is not in the glossary, say so rather than inventing a definition,
   and offer the closest covered term if one genuinely applies.

## Terms users ask about most

The glossary covers 30 definitions including EIN, ITIN, Form 5472, W-8BEN,
and nexus. Anchor these with the verified facts below.

## Verified facts — use these, they override older copy

- EIN for non-residents: 12–18 days (faxed SS-4). Instant online EIN is for US
  residents only.
- Delaware annual LLC tax: $400/year.
- No BOI filing is required for US LLCs — if a definition or older source says
  US LLCs must file Beneficial Ownership Information, correct it.
- Form 5472: filed annually by foreign-owned single-member LLCs with a
  pro-forma Form 1120, even at $0 income. Penalty starts at $25,000.
- Form 1065 (multi-member LLC): for the 2025 tax year, due Monday,
  March 16, 2026.
- File federal returns every year even at $0 income.

## Answer style

- Plain English, no jargon stacked on jargon; define any second term you use.
- One term per answer unless the user asks for a comparison.
- Short: a definition, why it matters, and (only if covered) the deadline or
  form it connects to.
- Never invent stats, fees, or deadlines that are not in the glossary or the
  verified facts above.
