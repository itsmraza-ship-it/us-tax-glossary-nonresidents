# US Tax & Business Glossary for Non-Resident Founders

> **⚠️ Not tax advice.** Definitions are simplified for clarity. Verify anything
> you act on with a qualified cross-border tax professional.

---

### EIN (Employer Identification Number)
A nine-digit tax ID issued by the IRS to a US business entity. You need one to open a US bank account, apply to Stripe/PayPal, and file business taxes. Non-US founders without an SSN apply by fax or mail.

### ITIN (Individual Taxpayer Identification Number)
A tax ID for individuals who need a US tax number but aren't eligible for an SSN — e.g. a non-resident founder who must file a US individual return. Applied for with IRS Form W-7.

### LLC (Limited Liability Company)
A US business entity that combines limited liability (your personal assets are shielded) with flexible taxation. The most common choice for non-resident founders. Formed by filing Articles of Organization with a state.

### Disregarded entity
How the IRS treats a single-member LLC by default: the LLC is "disregarded" as separate from its owner for income tax, so profits flow to the owner's return. It does **not** mean "no filings" — see Form 5472.

### C-corporation
A corporation taxed as its own entity (pays corporate income tax; shareholders pay tax again on dividends — "double taxation"). The standard vehicle for startups raising US venture capital, usually formed in Delaware.

### S-corporation
A corporation that elects pass-through taxation (no corporate-level tax). **Nonresident aliens cannot be S-corp shareholders** — so this election is off the table for most non-US founders.

### Articles of Organization
The document filed with a state to create an LLC (called a Certificate of Formation in some states, e.g. Delaware). Includes the company name, registered agent, and organizer.

### Operating Agreement
The LLC's internal rulebook: who owns what, how decisions are made, how profits are split, what happens when someone leaves. Not filed with the state, but banks and partners often ask for it.

### Registered agent
A person or company with a physical address in your formation state, designated to receive legal and state documents on your company's behalf. Required by every state; the address is public record.

### Annual report
A yearly filing with the state confirming your company's details (address, agent, sometimes members). Deadlines and fees vary by state. Missing it can lead to penalties or administrative dissolution.

### Franchise tax
A state tax for the privilege of operating as a business entity there — not based on franchising. Delaware LLCs pay a flat annual tax; other states compute it differently or don't charge one at all.

### State filing fee
The one-time fee paid to the state when you file your Articles of Organization. 2026 figures: Wyoming $102, New Mexico $50, Delaware $90. Fees change — confirm current before filing.

### Foreign qualification
Registering your company to do business in a state other than where it was formed. If you form in Wyoming but operate in Texas, you likely need to foreign-qualify in Texas too.

### Nexus
A connection between your business and a state (physical presence, employees, inventory, or enough sales) that gives that state the right to tax or regulate you. Nexus is what triggers foreign qualification and sales-tax obligations.

### Form 1120
The US corporate income tax return. A foreign-owned single-member LLC (disregarded entity) files a **pro-forma** Form 1120 with no tax computation — purely as an attachment vehicle for Form 5472.

### Form 5472
An information return reporting transactions between a foreign-owned US entity and its foreign owner/related parties. Required yearly for foreign-owned single-member LLCs, attached to a pro-forma 1120. Penalties for missing it are steep — get professional help.

### Form 1040-NR
The US individual income tax return for nonresident aliens. Relevant if you personally have US-source income or filing obligations beyond the company.

### Form 1065
The partnership information return, filed by multi-member LLCs taxed as partnerships. Profits/losses pass through to members, who report them on their own returns.

### Schedule K-1 (Form 1065)
The statement each partner/member receives showing their share of the partnership's income, deductions, and credits — used to complete their own tax return.

### W-8BEN
A form a non-US **individual** gives to a US payer certifying foreign status, used to claim treaty benefits on withholding. Common when freelancers invoice US clients.

### W-8BEN-E
The entity version of W-8BEN — a foreign **company** certifies its status and claims treaty benefits. US platforms and clients may ask your LLC for this.

### W-9
The US-person equivalent: a US individual or entity provides their TIN to a payer. As a non-US founder you generally provide W-8 forms instead.

### Form 1099
Information returns US businesses file to report payments (e.g. 1099-NEC for contractor payments). Generally about payments **to** US persons — different rules apply for foreign payees.

### Withholding tax
Tax a US payer must withhold from certain payments to foreign persons (often 30% by default, reducible by treaty). Why clients ask you for W-8 forms.

### Tax treaty
An agreement between the US and another country that can reduce withholding rates or clarify which country taxes what. Whether it helps you depends on your country and income type.

### Effectively Connected Income (ECI)
Income from a US trade or business, taxed at graduated rates like a US person's income. Whether your LLC's income is ECI is a key — and technical — determination.

### FDAP (Fixed, Determinable, Annual, Periodical income)
Passive US-source income (interest, dividends, royalties, rents) — generally subject to flat 30% withholding (or a lower treaty rate), unlike ECI.

### Pass-through taxation
A tax regime where the entity itself pays no income tax; profits pass to owners who pay tax individually. Default for LLCs (single-member → disregarded; multi-member → partnership).

### Tax year / fiscal year
The 12-month period your books and tax filings cover. Most small LLCs use the calendar year (ending December 31).

### Estimated tax
Quarterly prepayments of income tax. If you'll owe US tax, you generally pay it in four installments during the year rather than one lump sum.

### Sales tax nexus
The threshold of activity in a state that obligates you to collect sales tax there. Selling digital products or SaaS? Rules vary wildly by state — check each state where you have customers or presence.

### DBA ("doing business as")
A registered alternate name under which your company operates. Optional; useful if your brand name differs from your legal LLC name.

### Beneficial ownership / BOI reporting
**Update:** the FinCEN final rule of August 11, 2026 permanently removed beneficial ownership information (BOI) reporting requirements for US companies and US persons. If you see old articles calling BOI a mandatory yearly filing, they're outdated.

### EIN Responsible Party
The individual the IRS recognizes as controlling the entity — required on the EIN application (Form SS-4). For a foreign-owned LLC, this is typically the owner.
